Actor-Based Execution on GPUs

Prof. Hyesoon Kim

May 2026 - Present
Georgia Tech
Research Details
  • Implemented the actor model on Vortex, an open-source RISC-V GPU, mapping one actor per hardware thread, and found global-memory message passing to consume 97% of cycles, causing a 9× slowdown against the non-actor baseline.
  • Traced the cost to scattered writes the coalescer cannot merge, leaving one DRAM write per message sent, and to the one-actor-per-thread mapping, which fixed the actor count to the machine size and left the GPU issuing at 6% of peak.
  • Proposed a message transport that appends each message with one L2 atomic, groups them by destination so a warp’s writes land together, sizes each group to fit the receiver’s cache, and drains one receiver’s queue per warp.
  • Achieved 35.8× and 25.6× speedups on Histogram and Index Gather over naive actor versions and 6.3× and 1.5× over their non-actor versions, and these gains grow as the problem scales.
  • Working on hardware/software co-design of lightweight send and receive instructions, and on generalizing the layer to other workloads dominated by fine-grained scattered communication.

Statically Bound Dataflow for Sparse Matrix Multiplication

Independent Research

Jun 2026 - Sep 2026
IIT Bombay
Research Details
  • Studied HiT, the state-of-the-art sparse matrix multiplication accelerator, and found its multipliers holding 25% of chip area and 37% of power while the units that pair and gather their operands hold 42% and 52%.
  • Traced this to intersection, a comparator array searching every cycle for nonzeros to share a lane and matching 0.12% of the time, and transport, a reduction so widely spread that nearly every partial sum rides a ring to its accumulator.
  • Realized the pattern is known offline, and proposed a dataflow that binds the schedule by longest-processing-time list scheduling, fixing which nonzero fills which lane each cycle, and folds a cluster’s 32-cell ring into a 4 by 8 grid.
  • Showed binding removes the comparator array, since the lanes now follow a fixed order rather than a search, and folding removes the ring and half the buffer ports, since partial sums now walk an 8-cell chain.
  • Modelled the design in a cycle-accurate simulator on SuiteSparse matrices and measured 3.08× over HiT on every sparse-sparse workload and 1.37× across the rest, on a 17.9% smaller chip drawing 19.4% less power in the sparse regime.

Efficient LUT-based Processing-using-Memory Architecture

Prof. Virendra Singh

Jan 2026 - Jun 2026
IIT Bombay
Research Details
  • Characterized 15 workloads across cryptography, signal processing, bioinformatics and machine learning on the state-of-the-art pLUTo architecture, and observed that LUT loading and row sweeping consume 95% of execution time.
  • Traced the overhead to two design limits, a subarray holding only one LUT while 11 of 15 workloads require over 20 LUTs, and a full row sweep on every query even though more than half of all LUT entries go unused in 9 of 15 workloads.
  • Proposed an architecture addressing both with Packing, which keeps multiple LUTs co-resident in a subarray to avoid reloads on function switches, and Masking, which skips row activations for LUT indices absent from the current input.
  • Estimated area, latency, and energy by extending the pLUTo simulator, synthesizing the mask-generation logic in Yosys, and modelling the broadcast network in CACTI 7 at 22nm, with circuit-level functionality verified in ngspice.
  • Demonstrated a 4.3× speedup, 7.4× energy reduction, and 46.5× EDP improvement over pLUTo at 14.2% area overhead, alongside 86.7× and 14.3× speedups over an Intel Core i9-14900 CPU and an NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPU.

Memory Hierarchy Design

Prof. Virendra Singh

Nov 2024 - Mar 2026
IIT Bombay
Research Details
1. Virtual-Real Cache Hierarchy
  • Studied the TLB energy of VIPT L1 caches, which translate on every access and draw up to 13% of core power, and found VIVT caches remove those lookups only to move the cost onto the L2 critical path as synonym handling.
  • Proposed a two-level hierarchy with a VIVT L1 backed by CBIT, a location table built from the L2 tag array.
  • Resolved each L1 miss by indexing CBIT with the translated physical address, whose valid and level bits flag the block as a synonym in L1, resident in L2, or absent, and whose way field gives a tag-free read from either cache.
  • Modelled the proposal in ChampSim and simulated it on 49 SPEC CPU2006 and CPU2017 traces as well as multi-threaded CloudSuite workloads in a quad-core setup, with energy and area derived from CACTI 6.5 at 32nm.
  • Improved performance by 8% over a VIPT baseline and 15% over prior-art VC-DSR, while cutting dynamic cache energy by up to 64% and replacing the 0.57mm² L2 tag array with a 0.21mm² CBIT.
2. Data-Aware Prefetching for Graph Workloads
  • Profiled the GAP kernels by data type and observed low-reuse structure blocks evicting higher-utility property data hierarchy-wide, limiting the L2 to a 16% average hit rate, low enough that removing it improved performance.
  • Traced this to pointer chasing through the CSR offset, structure, and property arrays, whose data-dependent load-load chains stride prefetchers cannot follow and recent prefetchers like Berti and Pythia cannot learn.
  • Proposed RAGE, which isolates structure data in a structure cache replacing the L2 and reserves L1 and LLC for property data, generating dependent property prefetches at the memory controller from structure prefetch returns.
  • Modelled RAGE on Sniper and simulated a 4-core system across five GAP kernels and eight graphs exceeding LLC capacity, with access latencies derived in CACTI and chip area in McPAT at 45nm.
  • Achieved a 24% average speedup over no prefetching, outperforming recent general-purpose prefetchers and leading the closest hardware graph prefetchers DROPLET and SDC+LP by 5% and 7%, at 0.03% chip area overhead.
3. Lightweight Cache Replacement
  • Studied the cost of LRU, whose recency state scales with associativity, and of LLC-based policies such as SHiP and Hawkeye, which add large predictor tables, leaving no low-overhead policy usable at all cache levels.
  • Proposed LiC, which reframes the problem from deciding which block to evict to deciding which block to protect, retaining the most recently accessed block of a set with a single log2 n-bit pointer per set in an n-way cache.
  • Modelled LiC on ChampSim and simulated it on 49 SPEC CPU2006 and CPU2017 single-core traces as well as multi-threaded CloudSuite workloads in a quad-core setup, with its storage and power estimated in CACTI at 45nm.
  • Matched LRU’s performance to within 0.001% while shrinking the replacement-policy storage of a 2MB 16-way LLC by 16×, 14×, and 28× against LRU, SHiP, and Hawkeye, and by 4× against low-cost policies NRU and PLRU.
