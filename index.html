CPU Architecture for Efficient LLM Inference

Prof. Jongse Park

Oct 2025 - Present
KAIST
Research Details

Profiling LLM serving across retrieval-augmented and agentic pipelines showed the CPU under 5% utilized and active only in short bursts, an opportunity to raise its utilization by handing it part of the inference work. Characterizing Llama3-8B and Qwen2-7B on an Intel Xeon Gold CPU revealed over-provisioned hardware: a bimodal predictor came within 1% IPC of TAGE, and prefill throughput held with the reorder buffer at 38% of its size.

The caches and hardware prefetchers were also found to capture little of the weight and KV-cache traffic, motivating a proposed CPU mode for LLM inference that trims this over-provisioned hardware and serves that traffic more efficiently. Ongoing work builds an RVV-enabled RISC-V model in Sniper, calibrated against the XiangShan core, to evaluate the performance and energy of the proposal against a general-purpose baseline.

Actor-Based Execution on GPUs

Prof. Hyesoon Kim

May 2026 - Present
Georgia Tech
Research Details

Implementing the actor model on Vortex, an open-source RISC-V GPU, with one actor per hardware thread revealed that global-memory message passing consumed 97% of cycles, making it 9× slower than the non-actor baseline. The cost came from two sources: scattered writes the coalescer cannot merge, leaving one DRAM write per message, and the one-actor-per-thread mapping, which tied the actor count to the machine size and left the GPU issuing at 6% of peak.

The proposed message transport appends each message with a single L2 atomic, groups messages by destination so a warp’s writes land together, sizes each group to fit the receiver’s cache, and drains one receiver’s queue per warp. It achieves 35.8× and 25.6× speedups on Histogram and Index Gather over naive actor versions, and 6.3× and 1.5× over their non-actor versions, with gains growing as the problem scales. Ongoing work explores hardware/software co-design of lightweight send and receive instructions, and on generalizing the layer to other workloads dominated by fine-grained scattered communication.

Statically Bound Dataflow for Sparse Matrix Multiplication

Independent Research

Jun 2026 - Sep 2026
IIT Bombay
Research Details

A study of HiT, the state-of-the-art sparse matrix multiplication accelerator, showed that its multipliers take only 25% of chip area and 37% of power, while the units that pair and gather their operands take 42% and 52%. This overhead comes from intersection, a comparator array that searches every cycle for nonzeros to share a lane yet matches only 0.12% of the time, and from transport, a reduction spread so widely that nearly every partial sum rides a ring to its accumulator.

Since the sparsity pattern is known offline, the proposed dataflow binds the schedule ahead of time using longest-processing-time list scheduling, fixing which nonzero fills which lane each cycle, and folds each cluster’s 32-cell ring into a 4×8 grid. Binding removes the comparator array, as lanes now follow a fixed order rather than a search, and folding removes the ring and half the buffer ports, as partial sums now walk an 8-cell chain. In a cycle-accurate simulator on SuiteSparse matrices, the design is 3.08× faster than HiT on every sparse-sparse workload and 1.37× faster across the rest, on a 17.9% smaller chip drawing 19.4% less power in the sparse regime.

Efficient LUT-based Processing-using-Memory Architecture

Prof. Virendra Singh

Jan 2026 - Jun 2026
IIT Bombay
Research Details

Characterizing 15 workloads across cryptography, signal processing, bioinformatics, and machine learning on the state-of-the-art pLUTo architecture, we observed that LUT loading and row sweeping consume 95% of execution time. This traces to two design limits: a subarray holds only one LUT while 11 of 15 workloads need more than 20, and every query sweeps the full row even though more than half of all LUT entries go unused in 9 of 15 workloads.

We proposed an architecture that addresses both through Packing, which keeps multiple LUTs co-resident in a subarray to avoid reloads on function switches, and Masking, which skips row activations for LUT indices absent from the current input. We evaluated it by extending the pLUTo simulator, synthesizing the mask-generation logic in Yosys, modelling the broadcast network in CACTI 7 at 22nm, and verifying circuit-level functionality in ngspice. It delivers a 4.3× speedup, 7.4× energy reduction, and 46.5× EDP improvement over pLUTo at 14.2% area overhead, along with 86.7× and 14.3× speedups over an Intel Core i9-14900 CPU and an NVIDIA RTX 4070 GPU.

Memory Hierarchy Design

Prof. Virendra Singh

Nov 2024 - Mar 2026
IIT Bombay
Research Details
1. Virtual-Real Cache Hierarchy

VIPT L1 caches translate on every access, and their TLB lookups draw up to 13% of core power; VIVT caches remove those lookups but move the cost onto the L2 critical path as synonym handling. We proposed a two-level hierarchy with a VIVT L1 backed by CBIT, a location table built from the L2 tag array. On an L1 miss, CBIT is indexed with the translated physical address: its valid and level bits indicate whether the block is a synonym in L1, resident in L2, or absent, and its way field enables a tag-free read from either cache. Modelled in ChampSim on 49 SPEC CPU2006 and CPU2017 traces and multi-threaded CloudSuite workloads on a quad-core system, with energy and area from CACTI 6.5 at 32nm, the design improves performance by 8% over a VIPT baseline and 15% over prior-art VC-DSR, cuts dynamic cache energy by up to 64%, and replaces the 0.57mm² L2 tag array with a 0.21mm² CBIT.

2. Data-Aware Prefetching for Graph Workloads

Profiling the GAP kernels by data type, we observed low-reuse structure blocks evicting higher-utility property data throughout the hierarchy, limiting the L2 to a 16% average hit rate, so low that removing the L2 improved performance. The root cause is pointer chasing through the CSR offset, structure, and property arrays, whose data-dependent load-load chains stride prefetchers cannot follow and recent prefetchers like Berti and Pythia cannot learn. We proposed RAGE, which isolates structure data in a structure cache that replaces the L2, reserves L1 and LLC for property data, and generates dependent property prefetches at the memory controller from structure prefetch returns. Simulated in Sniper on a 4-core system across five GAP kernels and eight graphs exceeding LLC capacity, with latencies from CACTI and area from McPAT at 45nm, RAGE achieves a 24% average speedup over no prefetching, outperforms recent general-purpose prefetchers, and leads the closest hardware graph prefetchers, DROPLET and SDC+LP, by 5% and 7% at 0.03% chip area overhead.

3. Lightweight Cache Replacement

LRU’s recency state grows with associativity, and LLC-oriented policies such as SHiP and Hawkeye add large predictor tables, leaving no low-overhead policy usable at every cache level. We proposed LiC, which reframes replacement from deciding which block to evict to deciding which block to protect, retaining the most recently accessed block of each set with a single log2n-bit pointer per set in an n-way cache. Modelled in ChampSim on 49 SPEC CPU2006 and CPU2017 single-core traces and multi-threaded CloudSuite workloads on a quad-core system, with storage and power from CACTI at 45nm, LiC matches LRU’s performance to within 0.001% while shrinking the replacement-policy storage of a 2MB 16-way LLC by 16×, 14×, and 28× relative to LRU, SHiP, and Hawkeye, and by 4× relative to the low-cost policies NRU and PLRU.

Set Associative 3D DRAM Caches

Prof. Biswabandan Panda

Aug 2024 - Nov 2024
IIT Bombay
Research Details

This work studied 3D DRAM technologies, including High Bandwidth Memory and the Hybrid Memory Cube, and analyzed SILO, which uses a private die-stacked DRAM cache similar to HMC. Building on this, a 64 MB HMC tag array for an HBM cache was implemented in ChampSim to enable set associativity in HBM, achieving an 8.5% improvement over a direct-mapped HBM cache and a 24.27% speedup over the baseline.
